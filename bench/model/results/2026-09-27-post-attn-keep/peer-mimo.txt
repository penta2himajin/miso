## 1) Verdict: re-profile first — no code until one fresh pass

- The only profile is **pre-KEEP and approximate**; KEEP #29 changed MoE-down layout/occupancy (now LDS9296, occ3/2) and #31 changed attn. The 39/32/10/8/6/4 split is stale, and our attribution error on it is **larger than the 2% gate** — you cannot pick a bet from it.
- Both KEEPs confirm the structural fact that decides everything: **the top two buckets (71%) are FETCH≈ideal**, i.e. those kernels are at their byte roofline. So the only remaining wins are (a) the non-fetch residual (launch/gap, RMSNorm, LM head, DeltaNet) and (b) **fewer bytes per output token**. A stale profile can't tell you which of those owns the 146.
- One rocprof run answers the deciding question: *is wall ≈ Σkernel + gaps, and is each big kernel at peak HBM?* Pre-commit to the branches so profiling doesn't become a stall: **gaps ≥3% of short wall → bet #2; KV ≥~19% of 32k wall → bet #3; neither → go straight to bet #1's acceptance measurement.** pp512 1439.8 and ctx17 144.8 are guardrails, not targets.

## 2) Ranked 3 new bets

**#1 — Speculative verify: k candidate tokens per weight pass**
- **Mechanism:** short decode is weight-fetch-bound, so throughput is set by bytes read per *output token*. Verifying k candidates in one forward re-reads the dense weights ~once and amortizes them over k accepted tokens — a byte-side multiplier that touches nothing in-kernel.
- **Why not reopen REJECT:** orthogonal to every closed item — not GEMV xn FP16 (#30), not GEMV ISA schedule, not gate-up txn layout (layout untouched; only *how often* it's read), not M8b SlotW/Split-K/down-LDS, not coop top-k, not consumer RMSNorm, not FP16 Q/fdot2, not attn_n_splits, not BLAS/MFMA/atomics/megakernel.
- **Measure-first:** zero code — log per-position acceptance for small-k on short prompts and compute predicted e2e before writing anything.
- **Sensitivity (the key insight):** ceiling is set by MoE, not acceptance. At top-2 × k=3 tokens you touch ~4.6 of 8 experts ≈ **2.3× the MoE bytes**, and the LM head is scored k times (8%); dense amortizes fully, MoE inflates. Net ≈ **1.3–1.5× (≈190–220 tok/s)** rather than ideal k×; dies below ~0.5 accept.
- **Risk:** highest of the three — engineering size, unknown acceptance, quality parity, and the harness must count only accepted tokens. It is also the **only** bet with enough headroom to reach 190 at all.

**#2 — Cross-kernel seam fusion / launch-gap elimination (short)**
- **Mechanism:** fuse exactly two seams — RMSNorm(+residual) into the following GEMM's prologue and the preceding GEMM's epilogue — keeping the normalized hidden in LDS/registers. Per seam you delete one launch, one wave-drain, and one global round trip; ~2 seams × ~30 layers per token, against 6.8 ms/token wall, so 5–8 µs each is worth a few percent.
- **Why not reopen REJECT:** not "consumer RMSNorm×65" (execution unit and wave count of the norm unchanged — only traffic and launches removed), not a megakernel (bounded pairwise fusion, layer semantics intact), not GEMV ISA schedule (no intra-kernel instruction reordering).
- **Measure-first:** rocprof timeline — Σkernel vs wall, launches/token, summed gap time. **Kill it if gaps + launch overhead <2% of wall.**
- **Sensitivity:** win = gap share × recoverable fraction; 6% gaps recovered 50% → +3% short, gate cleared. If kernels are already back-to-back, dead on arrival.
- **Risk:** low–medium — seam numerics (residual/norm order) and the ≤1% regression rule; chief risk is the win being ~0.

**#3 — KV-cache byte compression (int8/per-channel packed K,V) → the 32k gate**
- **Mechanism:** KEEP #31 removed *redundant* K/V reads inside a workgroup (LDS overlay), not total KV bytes. Pack KV 2:1 and dequant in registers into the existing overlay → halve context-proportional traffic. From the brief: 1/112.2 − 1/134.2 = 1.46 ms = 7 × KV4k → KV4k ≈ 0.21 ms → **KV at 32k ≈ 1.67 ms ≈ 19% of wall**. Halving it ≈ **+9% at 32k (112→~123)**, and ctx17 is unaffected, so **no short regression by construction** — exactly the ≥5% long-only gate.
- **Why not reopen REJECT:** the rejects are attn *compute*-side (FP16 Q/fdot2, more attn_n_splits, LDS split variants). KV *storage format* is untouched by all of them, and #31's overlay is complementary, not superseded.
- **Measure-first:** ctx32k rocprof — attn kernel share, achieved HBM BW vs peak, actual KV bytes read; confirm bandwidth-bound first.
- **Sensitivity:** scales with KV bytes/total (GQA-dependent). If the profile shows KV <12% of 32k wall, it falls below the 5% gate; every cycle of dequant is deducted from bytes saved.
- **Risk:** medium — long-context quality (attention is error-sensitive on K/V), plus it must not leak into the short path.

*Scope note: #1 is algorithmic. If only in-kernel bets are admissible, substitute LM-head bytes + final-norm/sampler seam fusion (8%) and a head-parallel DeltaNet state update (6%, serial recurrence at bs=1) in its place.*

## 3) Do-not-try

- GEMV xn FP16 (#30); any GEMV ISA-schedule reshuffle; M8b SlotW / Split-K / drop-down-LDS variants; gate-up weight txn layout — **FETCH≈ideal closes the entire fetch-side family, so no re-tune of tile/schedule/layout can pay.**
- Co-op top-k; consumer-side RMSNorm×65 (any execution-unit/wave-count move of the norm); FP16 Q / fdot2; more `attn_n_splits` alone; further attn LDS-split variants (the overlay already owns that LDS).
- BLAS/MFMA/atomics, and the megakernel (it would also subsume and endanger seam fusion).
- Also avoid: re-opening MoE-down's KEEP #29 format without a byte measurement, occupancy churn for its own sake, and spending a KEEP on the ~4% short attn bucket.

## 4) attn tile/head split after KEEP #31

**No — after the K/V LDS overlay, further tile/head splitting buys nothing at short (attn is ~4% there) and at 32k the binding constraint is KV *bytes*, not how the tiles are cut, so split further only if the fresh profile shows the overlay LDS-bound, otherwise change the KV storage format instead.**
